Can Telegram read your files? What taasant protects, and what it does not

By Vedant · 7 October 2026

Short answer: no. Telegram stores what taasant sends, and what taasant sends is already encrypted.

The longer answer is more useful, because "encrypted" gets said about a lot of things that turn out to be less private than they sound.

Where the encryption happens

A chat with a bot is an ordinary cloud chat. Telegram encrypts it on the way to its servers, but the servers hold the keys, so Telegram could read what is in it. Only Secret Chats are end-to-end encrypted, and bots cannot use those.

So taasant does not rely on Telegram for privacy at all. Files are encrypted on your computer, before upload, with AES-256-GCM from Go's standard library. I did not write my own encryption, and nobody should. The key is 32 random bytes that taasant keygen prints once. It stays on your computer.

What Telegram can see

  • How many messages your bot sends, and when.
  • The size of each part, so roughly how big your files are.
  • Which Telegram account made the bot.
  • Your IP address, as with any other use of Telegram.

What Telegram cannot see

  • What is in the files. Without the key the parts are noise.
  • What the files are called. Every attachment is named part.bin. The real names live in a list on your computer.
  • Which parts belong together. The list that ties parts into a file is encrypted too.

A changed or damaged part is also caught. GCM checks every part when it is decrypted, and a part that does not match stops the download.

What can still go wrong

Encryption solves one problem. These are the ones it does not solve.

  • You lose the key. Then the files are gone. There is no reset link and nobody to ask, me included. Put a copy in a password manager on the day you make it.
  • Someone gets the key. It sits in your shell's settings file, so anyone who can read the files of your user account can read it. The key alone is not enough, though. They would also need the encrypted files, which means your bot or your Telegram account.
  • Someone gets only the bot token. They could act as your bot and make a mess. They could not read your files, because the token does not decrypt anything.
  • Telegram stops keeping the files. A banned account, a removed bot, a change of policy. This is free storage and it comes with no promise.
  • You lose the list of files. It lives on one computer. Without it the files are still in the chat, but taasant no longer knows which messages are which. Copy the taas folder somewhere safe now and then.

Has anyone checked the code?

Not professionally. taasant is a hobby project and has had no security audit. What it does have is public source code under the MIT license, a couple of thousand lines of Go with the tests included. The encryption is one file of about thirty lines, tgstore/crypto.go. Read it. If you find a problem, open an issue.

So, is it safe?

For a second, encrypted copy of your own files, I think it is a reasonable choice, and that is the job it is designed for.

For the only copy of something you cannot lose, or for anything where a person's safety depends on it staying secret, use something that has been audited and that someone is paid to keep running.
